ScienceHigh priority

Circuits: series, parallel and resistance

Revision summary

A circuit needs a complete conducting path from one terminal of a power supply, through components, and back to the other terminal. In a series circuit, components share a single path: the current is the same at every point and adding a lamp usually increases total resistance. In a parallel circuit, current has more than one path, and each branch is connected across the same supply voltage. A branch can continue operating when another branch is broken. Ohm’s Law relates voltage, current and resistance: V = I × R. For example, 6 V across 3 Ω gives 2 A.

Try this before your test

Sketch two bulbs in one loop, then sketch the same bulbs on separate branches. Identify which arrangement allows one lamp to remain on when the other fails.

ScienceHigh priority

Digestion and body systems

Revision summary

Digestion breaks large food molecules into smaller substances that can be absorbed. Teeth and muscular mixing carry out mechanical digestion; enzymes carry out chemical digestion. The stomach churns food and begins protein digestion. Most digestion and nutrient absorption occur in the small intestine. The large intestine absorbs water from material that remains. Incisors cut, canines tear and premolars/molars crush and grind food.

Try this before your test

Trace food from mouth to stomach, small intestine and large intestine. State where most nutrients enter the body and how that differs from water absorption in the large intestine.

ScienceNeeds consolidation

Cells and microscopy

Revision summary

The cell is the basic unit of living things. Both plant and animal cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus (in typical eukaryotic cells), mitochondria and ribosomes. Plant cells also commonly have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissue and a large central vacuole. Chloroplasts capture light for photosynthesis; mitochondria help release energy from food through cellular respiration. Magnification equals image size divided by actual size, using consistent units.

Try this before your test

Compare a leaf cell and an animal cheek cell. Name two structures found in both and two characteristic plant structures.

ScienceNeeds consolidation

Scientific investigations and fair tests

Revision summary

A fair test changes one independent variable and measures a dependent variable while keeping important controlled variables the same. A hypothesis is a testable prediction; evidence comes from recorded observations and measurements. A good table includes clear headings and units. Graphs should put the independent variable on the horizontal axis and the dependent variable on the vertical axis in common simple experiments. A conclusion describes what the evidence shows, while acknowledging limitations.

Try this before your test

For a test of how light affects plant growth, identify the light level as the independent variable, plant growth as the dependent variable, and water, species and soil as controls.

FrenchHigh priority

Appearance, personality and adjective agreement

Revision summary

Many French descriptive adjectives agree with the noun they describe. A common pattern is to add -e for the feminine and -s for the plural, but there are exceptions. Examples: Il est petit / Elle est petite; Ils sont petits / Elles sont petites. Some adjectives change more: sportif → sportive; heureux → heureuse. Useful adjectives include grand/grande (tall), timide (shy), drôle (funny), gentil/gentille (kind). In sentences such as “Elle a les cheveux bruns”, hair colour also agrees with “cheveux” (masculine plural).

Try this before your test

Translate “She is shy”, “He is kind” and “They (feminine) are sporty.” Check endings, not just vocabulary.

FrenchNeeds consolidation

Family and school subjects

Revision summary

Useful family words: la mère (mother), le père (father), le frère (brother), la sœur (sister), les parents (parents), la famille (family). French nouns normally have grammatical gender; articles such as le, la, l’ and les reflect gender and number. School examples include le français, les maths, les sciences and l’histoire. To say what you study, use “J’étudie les sciences” or “J’ai cours de français.” To express a preference: “J’aime l’histoire.”

Try this before your test

Write five pairs of articles and nouns, then say which subject you like and which you do not like.

FrenchNeeds consolidation

Classroom, time and routines

Revision summary

For time, “Il est huit heures” means it is eight o’clock; “Il est huit heures et demie” is 8:30. Useful routine verbs include se lever (get up), prendre le petit déjeuner (eat breakfast), aller à l’école (go to school) and faire ses devoirs (do homework). Reflexive verbs change with the subject: “Je me lève à sept heures.” Frequency words include toujours (always), souvent (often), parfois (sometimes) and jamais (never). For plans, “Je vais étudier” means I am going to study.

Try this before your test

Put three routine activities in chronological order and write the time of each in French. Distinguish “je vais” as movement from “je vais + infinitive” as a near-future construction.

HumanitiesPriority humanities topic

Population, the DTM and development

Revision summary

A population pyramid shows the numbers or percentages of people in age groups, normally separated by sex. A broad base suggests many children and relatively high recent birth rates; a narrow base can indicate fewer births. The Demographic Transition Model describes changes in birth and death rates over time. In stage 1 both are high; in stage 2 death rates decline while births remain high; in stage 3 birth rates fall; in stage 4 both rates are low. Some versions add a fifth stage in which births remain below deaths. The Human Development Index (HDI) combines health, education and income indicators. It does not simply rank countries by total GDP.

Try this before your test

Compare two countries: one with high life expectancy, many years of schooling and higher income, and one with lower values. Explain why HDI considers more than money.

HumanitiesSupplementary source

DTM — Visual reference

Revision summary

The Demographic Transition Model can be easier to understand as a graph. Focus on how the distance between birth-rate and death-rate lines affects population growth. If births remain high and deaths fall, population grows rapidly; when both rates become low, population growth slows. The model is a simplification and countries do not always follow identical pathways.

Try this before your test

Use the source diagram to identify the stage where population tends to grow fastest, and explain why.
