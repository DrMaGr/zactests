Learn to answer an evaluative history question

In Humanities, a strong response does more than describe what happened. It makes a judgement, uses accurate historical evidence, explains why it matters, and weighs a competing view.

P — Point

State an argument that answers the question.

E — Evidence

Use a precise event, date, person or consequence.

E — Explain

Show how the evidence proves your claim.

L — Link

Return to the question and your overall judgement.

For “to what extent”, “discuss” and “most significant”: compare at least two factors, acknowledge a reasonable counterargument, and finish with a clear judgement. A PEEL paragraph is one part of an extended response—not the whole essay.
Assessment rule: this module does not automatically grade the quality of historical arguments. It records writing and provides a self-review rubric for Zac, a parent or a teacher to assess.
