What is PEEL?

PEEL is a way to make one clear argument in a paragraph. It stops an exam answer becoming a list of facts or a retelling of the story.

PPoint

Answer the question with one clear idea. What are you arguing?

EEvidence

Give a specific event, action or detail from the novel that supports the point.

EExplanation

Explain how that evidence proves the point, and what it suggests.

LLink

Return to the question or connect your idea to the novel's wider message.

Remember: Evidence tells the examiner what happens. Explanation tells them why it matters. Both are essential.

Worked example from The Giver

Question: How does Lowry show that Jonas's community limits people's freedom?

POINTLowry suggests that the community limits freedom by controlling major decisions in people's lives.
EVIDENCEFor example, at the Ceremony of Twelve, the Elders choose each child's future job.
EXPLANATIONBecause the children cannot choose their own paths, their personal ambitions matter less than the community's need for order.
LINKLowry therefore encourages readers to question whether a perfectly organised society is worth sacrificing individual choice.
Why is this stronger than retelling? "The Elders assign jobs" only states a fact. "This removes the children's ability to choose their futures" explains how the fact supports an argument about freedom.

The example uses a paraphrase of a novel event, not a direct quotation. In school, follow your teacher's instructions about quotations and references.
